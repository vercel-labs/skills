# Style and Structure

Use this reference when rewriting Science-family manuscript prose.

## Writing stance

Write for scientists outside the immediate subfield. The prose should be precise enough for
experts and legible enough for adjacent disciplines.

Prioritize:

- Broad significance before narrow technical detail.
- Active verbs and clear agents.
- Concrete evidence over promotional adjectives.
- Short definitions for necessary terms before acronym-heavy passages.
- Direct claim-to-figure mapping.
- Results before speculation.

Avoid:

- Claiming priority unless it is necessary and defensible.
- Ending with vague future-work promises.
- Abstracts that start with a specialized acronym or technique.
- Discussion text that repeats the results without explaining consequence.
- Cover-letter language that oversells the work instead of helping the editor judge fit.

## Title

A Science-style title should make the advance visible without turning into a full sentence of
technical detail.

Checklist:

- Names the biological, physical, computational, or conceptual object of the paper.
- Signals the advance or mechanism when space allows.
- Avoids unexplained acronyms and local project names.
- Does not overstate causality, generality, or clinical readiness.

## One-sentence summary

Use when the target journal asks for it. Make it a compact statement of the main result, not a
second title and not a methods sentence.

Pattern:

```text
[Main discovery/advance] reveals/enables/explains [broad consequence] in [system/context].
```

## Abstract

Build a one-paragraph abstract around four moves:

1. Accessible opening question or gap.
2. Study-specific approach or system.
3. Main results in logical order.
4. Concluding consequence bounded by the evidence.

Keep the first sentence understandable without the reader knowing the subfield's acronyms.
Use numerical results only when they are central to the claim and interpretable without context.

## Introduction

Keep the introduction short and functional:

- Paragraph 1: field-level problem and why it matters.
- Paragraph 2: unresolved gap and why current approaches are insufficient.
- Scope sentence: what this paper does and what evidence will decide the question.

Do not write a full literature review unless the article type requires it.

## Results

Every results block should have:

- A question or test.
- The evidence generated.
- The result.
- The direct interpretation.
- A citation to the figure/table carrying the evidence.

Use subheadings only if the article type and target journal allow them or the draft benefits from
reviewer-readable structure.

## Discussion or interpretation

Tie the evidence back to the central advance:

- State what changed because of the results.
- Explain limits without undermining established evidence.
- Distinguish demonstrated findings from plausible implications.
- Avoid broad translational, clinical, environmental, or commercial claims unless the evidence
  directly supports them.

## Cover letter

The cover letter should help the editor triage:

- Manuscript title and target journal.
- Main finding in one or two concrete sentences.
- Why the result matters beyond the immediate specialty.
- Why this Science-family journal is the right venue.
- Related/competing manuscripts, preprints, conflicts, or timing concerns.
- Suggested and excluded reviewers when requested by the submission system.

Keep it concise and factual.
